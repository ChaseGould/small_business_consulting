# Marketing Ideas

Notes from strategy discussion (July 2026). These are internal ideas for how to
market TaskDepot AI's services. Nothing here goes on the public site.

## Core positioning

- We are not selling three services (automation, web dev, AI image/video), we
  are selling one outcome: "we make small businesses look bigger and run leaner
  than they can afford to on their own."
- The three services form a single marketing pipeline, not a menu:
  - AI image/video content drives customers in (top of funnel)
  - The website converts them (middle)
  - Automation handles them when they arrive (bottom)
- Avoid the commodity label "web developer" in pitches. It invites price
  comparison against Fiverr, Wix, and someone's nephew. Instead pitch the
  combination: "a month of scroll-stopping video ads AND a site that converts
  the clicks." Almost nobody local offers both sides.
- AI is the cost story, not the headline. Small business owners don't care
  that it's AI, they care that it's $500 instead of $5,000. Generation is
  cheap for us; that's why we can undercut a traditional agency on content
  volume.
- Open positioning question (revisit later, after real client signal): is
  TaskDepot AI a tech brand that also does marketing, or a marketing brand
  powered by tech? Leaning toward leading with marketing outcomes (customers,
  calls, foot traffic) and letting "AI + web dev" be the how. Small business
  owners buy customers, not technology. The current site leans tech-first.

## Tactics

### Spec ads (highest leverage)

- Pick 3 to 5 real local businesses without asking permission first.
- Make a spec AI video ad for each and send it to the owner: "Made this for
  you, it's yours free either way. If you want a month of these, here's my
  price."
- The deliverable and the advertisement for our services are the same
  artifact. Even ignored, the spec ads become the portfolio reel.

### Web dev before/afters

- Screenshot an ugly local business site, show the redesign next to it.
  Boring but proven.
- Pairs naturally with spec-ad outreach: "your ad is great but it's sending
  people to a site from 2011."

### Post the process, not just results

- Short clips like "watch me turn this taqueria's menu photo into a 15-second
  ad" perform well and position us as the local AI-marketing person before
  anyone is paying.
- One workflow, filmed once, becomes both content and lead gen.

### Productize at least one offer

- Fixed package with a visible price, e.g. "Starter package: 1-page site +
  8 social videos + Google Business setup, $X, 2 weeks."
- Small business owners freeze at "custom quotes." A fixed package is easier
  to market, easier to say yes to, and easier to fulfill repeatedly.

## Guardrails / reminders

- First clients come from direct outreach (local businesses we know, Facebook
  groups, walking into shops, past coworkers), not from people finding the
  site. The site's only job is to not embarrass us when a prospect checks us
  out.
- Rule of thumb: no website edits until 10 outreach contacts have been made.
  Building websites is the procrastination loop; outreach is the work.
- The site itself is the web dev portfolio piece, and an AI-made promo video
  on the page can be the video portfolio piece. The new services demo
  themselves.
- Let the first few clients decide the niche. If everyone bites on video,
  that's when a dedicated brand might earn its existence, funded by revenue
  and informed by real demand instead of a guess.
